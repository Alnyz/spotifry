Logo H, refined by the rules

The original H on the left, then three refined versions built on the Android icon grid. Below them, the checks a good logo should pass, run on version H2.

What I changed and why

  • Built on a grid, not by eye. Every part sits inside Android's safe circle (66 of 108 units), so no phone can cut it off. The bars and the gaps between them follow one rhythm, and the bars, handle and pan corners share one roundness. Shapes that repeat one measure read as calm and deliberate instead of hand-placed.
  • Fewer parts. Five bars became four, and they float as a sound wave around one centre line, so the shape cannot be mistaken for fingers. The pan is one solid shape and the handle one line. Fewer parts are easier to remember and survive at small sizes.
  • Upward rhythm. The bars swell and fade like one puff of steam, biggest just right of centre, and the handle tilts slightly up. Rising shapes read as energy and lift. The rounded ends keep it friendly instead of sharp.
  • A hidden second meaning (H2). A play button is cut out of the pan, in the empty space. You see the pan first and the play button on a second look. This is the same trick as the arrow hidden in the FedEx logo: it makes the logo stick in memory.
  • Colour with a job (H3). Blue says calm and trust, which fits a player with no ads. H3 makes the steam warm, the colour of heat, so the fry joke reads even faster. That adds a second colour to the app's one-accent rule, so it is your call.

Checks on H2

Simple and memorable: one colour, no detail

A good mark still reads as a flat silhouette in one colour. If you can recognise it as a black shape, it will print, stamp and embroider.

Versatile: works at every size

From the notification bar (about 24 px) up to the home screen. The bars must not merge into a block at the smallest size.

Safe on every phone

Android cuts icons into circles, squircles or rounded squares. The dashed circle is the area that is never cut; the whole mark stays inside it.

Appropriate and timeless

A pan and a sound meter say "fry" and "music" without words. No gradients, glow or 3D effects, which date fast. It stays clear of Spotify: no green, no circle with three curved lines.
